Reading aquarium water tests with computer vision

Case study · Water Test Reader (Android) · ← nrcode.com

Context

Aquarium hobbyists track water chemistry (nitrate, phosphate, pH, ...) with colorimetric test kits: you mix a sample with reagents and compare the resulting color against a printed reference scale. Reading that comparison by eye is subjective — lighting, screen calibration and the observer all shift the result, and the difference between two adjacent reference values can matter a lot for tank health.

Water Test Reader replaces the eyeball comparison with a phone photo: point the camera at the test card, and the app detects the reference scale and the sample, measures the color, and reports where it falls on the scale.

Challenge

A phone photo is an uncontrolled input — variable lighting, angle, partial occlusion, reflections. Getting a reproducible ppm reading out of that means solving several problems before any color math is meaningful:

  • reliably locating the reference circles/rectangles and the water sample in the frame, regardless of card orientation
  • sampling color from a patch that's actually representative, not a glare spot or a shadow
  • correcting for the ambient lighting's own color cast, which otherwise swamps the signal
  • knowing when a reading is untrustworthy instead of confidently returning a wrong number

Approach

  • an on-device YOLOv11 segmentation model (TFLite) detects the reference scale and the water sample directly in the photo — no manual cropping or alignment by the user
  • color is measured in LAB space for perceptual accuracy, with sampling designed to reject noisy or unstable regions of the photo
  • a matching step projects the measured color onto the reference scale and validates that the reading is actually trustworthy, rather than silently returning a best guess
  • a correction step compensates for the ambient lighting's own color cast before matching
  • the domain layer is built around a typed result model (success/failure with explicit reasons), keeping detection and measurement logic decoupled from the UI, in their own Kotlin modules
  • the color science was tuned empirically against known reference solutions rather than assumed up front

Results

  • a working end-to-end pipeline: photo → detection → color measurement → matched reference position with an uncertainty estimate
  • on-device inference, no photo ever leaves the phone
  • shipped on the Play Store, currently focused on the best-validated test kit with more brands and test types planned
